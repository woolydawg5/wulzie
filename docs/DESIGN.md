# Wulzie design rules

These rules come from Wooly's hands-on time with SLOOP (2026-11-11; his words are in
[PROMPTS.md](PROMPTS.md)). Every screen and feature is checked against them.

## Keep (what SLOOP gets right)

1. **Colour carries meaning.** The interface is colourful, and each track and each kind of thing
   has its own colour.
2. **The sound never stops.** While a loop plays you can tweak its sound, and nothing you do in a
   menu interrupts the audio.
3. **Play over your own loop.** A looping track's keys stay playable, so you can jam on top of
   what you recorded.
4. **A deep arpeggiator.** Arp settings are a first-class feature, not an afterthought.
5. **The winning recipe works early.** Reese bass + phaser + arpeggio + dubstep drums should be
   reachable as soon as the roadmap allows.

## Fix (where Wulzie should be better)

6. **Sound design goes deeper, and stays live.** More reese parameters than SLOOP offers, all
   tweakable while playing. Any sound you land on can become a new preset in one gesture.
7. **Saving is obvious.** Draft model, to be confirmed with Wooly:
   - There are only two things to save: a **SOUND** (one instrument's settings) and a **PROJECT**
     (everything: all tracks, loops, sounds and settings).
   - The project **saves itself**. You never lose work by forgetting.
   - Saving a sound is one gesture: hold SAVE, tap a key to pick the slot. The screen says in
     plain words what was saved and where.
   - Nothing is ever overwritten without the screen asking first.
8. **The sequencer is play-first.** You record by playing in time over the loop. Step editing is
   there for fixing and fine detail, not the only way in. The tutorial teaches it.
